What we run
Services
Everything the campus depends on, engineered for redundancy and least-privilege access.

  

    
🔄
Redundant Gateways
HSRPv2 virtual gateways on BLD1 and BLD2 with interface tracking and preemption. BLD1 is the active gateway and STP root.

    
🛡
Zone-Based Firewall
INSIDE, SERVERS and OUTSIDE zones. Only the services each zone needs are allowed to cross.

    
🔑
Centralized AAA
RADIUS authentication for wireless and network access. One identity, enforced the same way everywhere.

    
📶
Controller-Based Wi-Fi
A wireless controller manages two lightweight APs that broadcast three SSIDs, each mapped to its own VLAN.

    
🌐
Dynamic Routing
OSPF between the core and the firewall, tuned with interface costs so traffic takes the right path.

    
📞
Voice
Dedicated voice VLAN with call control and registered IP phones, separated from data.

Self-service
Help Center
Search common questions.

  
  

    
I can see the Wi-Fi networks but cannot join one
Secured networks need your credentials saved in a profile on the device. Create a profile with the exact SSID name, choose WPA2-Enterprise for Client and Executive (username and password) or WPA2-Personal for Guest (passphrase), then connect from that profile.

    
I joined Wi-Fi but got a 169.254.x.x address
That means no DHCP lease was received. Reconnect, wait up to 30 seconds, then release and renew your address. If it persists, open a ticket with the SSID and your location.

    
Which network should I use?
Staff use ITSupport-Client. Leadership uses ITSupport-Executive. Visitors use ITSupport-Guest, which only reaches the internet and DNS.

    
Why can guests not reach internal servers?
The guest VLAN is isolated by an access list on the gateways. Guests may use DNS and the internet, but internal subnets are blocked by design.

    
What is HSRP and why does it matter?
Two core switches share one virtual gateway address. If the active switch or its uplink fails, the standby takes over so devices keep the same gateway with no changes.

    
Who manages the network?
The ITSupport network team. Management traffic lives on its own VLAN (999), separate from user traffic.
